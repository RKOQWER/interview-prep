The real diagnosis

1. Patterns as topics, not signals

You can implement BFS. But when a fresh problem arrives, nothing in your head maps the statement to BFS. That mapping is a separate, trainable skill - and it was never the thing you practiced.

2. Blocked practice hid it

Twenty sliding-window problems in a row trains execution, not recognition: the block already tells you the pattern. Real interviews never label the pattern. So from day one, most of your practice here is mixed.

3. No post-mortem

An attempt without a written takeaway repeats forever. Every miss goes in the error log with a mistake class and a scheduled redo.

The 60-second scan

Before every problem, and never skipping a step. We rehearse this until it is automatic.

  1. Constraints first: the n bound tells you the allowed complexity before anything else (table below)
  2. Input shape: sorted? graph? tree? matrix? string? each shape has default tools
  3. Output shape: "count ways" points at DP, "min/max" at DP or greedy, "feasible?" at greedy or binary search on answer, "pair/index" at hash or two pointers
  4. Keywords: the statement's exact words are a lookup table (below)
  5. Commit: write your top-2 candidates with reasons, then pick one and code. Skipping this step is how you end up 30 minutes into the wrong approach

The daily loop (60-90 min)

5 min

Recall

Yesterday's signal rows from memory, plus one pattern skeleton rebuilt from scratch.

30 min

Cold problem

1-2 problems from any topic and any difficulty. Full scan ritual first. Hard limit: 20 minutes without a hint, one hint maximum.

15 min

Finish or study

Finish with the hint, or compare against the editorial. Extract the reusable skeleton.

10 min

Post-mortem

Log it: mistake class, the signal you missed, redo date. Clear any due redos first.

Mastery gate (your rule): a wrong-pattern mistake (class A) triggers 3 extra problems on the correct pattern this week, before anything new. A shaky pattern is never left behind.

Difficulty is mixed every day, per your call: easy problems appear only as warm-ups, mediums and hards are the regular diet. Your algorithms are fine; selection is what we are training, and mixed difficulty keeps it honest.

Recognition drills (the actual fix)

Route or Solve

I give you 8-10 problem statements or titles. You name the top-2 candidate patterns and one reason for each. No code, 10 minutes. Score: first-choice correct.

Constraint math

10 rows of n bounds, you write the allowed complexity for each in 2 minutes, then we check against the table. This makes step 1 of the scan instant.

Blind roulette

Mixed problems from patterns you already covered, no hints about the topic. You commit to a pattern out loud before coding; a wrong call still gets solved with the right one, then logged.
